Understanding Qualification Scores

A simple guide for IDG Sales Intelligence — no technical background needed

When a tender is scanned from TED or UNGM, the system reads the public notice and gives you three kinds of scores. These are rule-based checks (not a chatbot guess). They help your team decide whether to pursue a job, review it manually, or pass.

1. Extraction Confidence

What it means

How complete the notice information is. It answers: “Did the portal give us enough basic facts to work with?”

This is not about AI being clever. It only measures whether key fields were present on the public notice when it was imported.

How it is calculated

  • Starts at 85% when all key fields are present
  • Goes down by 12% for each missing item among:
    • Country
    • Buyer (who published the tender)
    • Estimated contract value
    • Submission deadline
  • Never goes below 35%
Examples: All four fields present → 85% · Two fields missing → 61% · Four fields missing → 37%

2. Overall Score

What it means

One number from 0 to 100 that shows how strong the tender looks for IDG security work. Higher is better.

What adds to the score

Factor What we look for Points (approx.)
Service fit Words like security, guarding, surveillance, protection in the title or category Up to 35
Country Job is in an IDG target country 18 (or 8 if another country)
Buyer Contracting authority name is listed 12
Contract value Estimated value is shown on the notice 10
Deadline Enough time left to submit (more days = more points) Up to 15

What the recommendation means

  • Bid — Strong fit; good candidate to pursue
  • Review — Possible fit; a person should check before deciding
  • No Bid — Weak fit; usually not worth pursuing
A low score does not always mean the job is useless — it may mean the notice was missing details. Human review can still overturn the suggestion.

3. Dimension Scores

What it means

Nine separate bars, each from 0 to 100. They break the Overall Score into topics so you can see why a tender scored the way it did. Each bar has its own simple rules — see the table below for what score you get and when.

Bar name What it measures How the score is set (0–100)
Service Match How closely the tender wording matches IDG security services (guarding, surveillance, protection, etc.) The system counts security-related words in the title and category.
3+ words → 100 · 2 words → ~86 · 1 word → ~57 · none → 0
Geography Whether the job country is an IDG priority market Target IDG country → 100
Another country listed → 55
Country missing → 0
Industry How well the tender fits IDG’s service sector Usually Service Match + 10 (capped at 100)
If no service match at all → 20
Technology Whether the notice mentions IT or digital systems +25 points for each IT-related word found (e.g. software, IT, digital, cloud, network)
Maximum → 100
Security Clearance Whether the word “security” appears in the notice (not the same as real government clearance) “Security” found in title or category → 80
Not found → 35
Framework Whether we know where the work will take place Country is listed → 60
Country missing → 25
Contract Value Whether a contract value was published on the notice Value shown → 70
Value missing → 35
Deadline How much time is left before the submission deadline 21+ days left → 85
7–20 days left → 55
Less than 7 days → 20
Deadline already passed → 5
Deadline not listed → 20
Strategic Customer Whether the beneficiary country is a strategic IDG market Target IDG country → 75
Other or unknown → 40

Reading the bars together

  • High Service Match + low Geography — Good security job, possibly wrong country.
  • High Security Clearance + low Service Match — The word “security” appears, but the job may not be core guarding work.
  • Low Contract Value + low Deadline — Notice is missing money and/or time details; check the source portal manually.
Tip: Dimension scores explain the shape of the opportunity. The Overall Score (section 2) is still the main number used for Bid / Review / No Bid.
